# Vega OS seals the 2025 Echo Show 8

**No.** As of October 2026 — eleven months after the Echo Show 8 2025 release (4th gen, internal codename "madeleine", model MD6NE4, AZ3 Pro, Vega OS, Alexa+) reached customers — **no public exploit, unlock, sideload, root, or hardware attack of any kind exists for it or its sibling Echo Show 11**, an absence verified from at least six independent search angles across XDA, GitHub, Reddit, Hackaday, AFTVnews, and the jailbreak-guide ecosystem ([security notes](../research_notes/Echo%20Show%208%202025%20hackability/security_research.md)). And the specific goal — a 24/7 custom app with full microphone and camera access — is **impossible on stock firmware by architecture**: the 5-mic array and 13 MP camera terminate exclusively inside Amazon's first-party Omnisense/Alexa stack, and no third-party surface (skill, web app, widget, or Alexa+ add-on) can touch raw audio or video, let alone autostart or persist. The device sits behind a triple barrier: brand-new silicon no one has torn down, a non-Android OS that deleted the entire ADB/fastboot/APK tooling surface every prior Echo exploit relied on, and **zero published security research on Vega OS in ~3.5 years in the field**. The only theoretical route is a Gen 3-style UART/eMMC hardware attack against its 8 GB eMMC — physically plausible, never attempted publicly, and missing the Android-userland payoff that made that method useful on the 2023 model. Where the earlier generations' fates were decided by silicon procurement accidents (Gen 1's exploitable MT8163 boot ROM, Gen 2's accidental MT8183 escape, Gen 3's fuse-locked-but-eMMC-writable Amlogic platform), the 2025 model is the first Show 8 that is **closed by design rather than by accident** ([previous report](Echo%20Show%208%20custom%20Linux.md)).

## Update (2026-10-12, overnight primary-source research)

Three claims in this report have since been superseded by direct artifact
analysis (full details: `../research_notes/Echo Show 8 2025 hackability/source_release_1571_findings.md`
and `fcc_and_rhodes_methodology.md`):

1. **The uninspected 1571 tarball has now been inspected.** The "one open
   door" analysis was right about its value: the mic array (TI TLV320ADC5140/
   5120 + ES7210/ES7243, raw 16-ch/96 kHz ALSA PCM) and camera (OV13858,
   standard V4L2) are ordinary Linux devices — the lock is purely Amazon's
   userspace. The adbd gate is fully decoded: a systemd fosflags condition on
   production builds plus an in-binary `/etc/os-release` `BUILD_VARIANT`
   check, with LAN adb behind one more plain file. Every gate is a file or an
   eMMC-stored flag — a Gen 3-style off-chip write could satisfy them all.
2. **The published defconfig has `CONFIG_KEXEC=y`, no module signing, and no
   dm-verity** — a materially softer kernel posture than Gen 3 (kexec
   compiled out). If root is ever gained, a custom kernel can be kexec'd at
   runtime, bypassing the fuse-locked boot chain entirely (shipped config
   still needs on-hardware verification).
3. **The FCC internal/external photo exhibits for 2A4DH-6274 (and the Show
   11's, 2A4DH-7824) are downloaded and sha-verified** — public since
   2026-03-29 but previously unnoticed by the community. The FCC unit is an
   EVT build ("NOT FOR SALE EVT G1"). Board-level probing is now supported by
   the Gen 3/Show 15 playbook (jcrasselt repo): UART at 921600 with an
   interruptible but command-blocked U-Boot; fastboot enumeration via
   Power+Vol-Down; eMMC via needle probes + EasyJTAG (1-bit SDR, 1 MHz, 1.8 V).

The bottom-line verdict is unchanged — nothing is publicly broken — but the
"if it comes, it looks like soldering" prediction now has a concrete,
source-verified target list and an arrival probe plan
(`Echo Show 8 2025 probe plan.md`).

## Eleven months on the market, zero public research

The verdict is an absence, and the absence is the finding. Since the November 12, 2025 launch of the Show 8 4th gen and Show 11 ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/amazon-echo-next-gen-lineup-3602556/)), no exploit claim, tool, ROM, TWRP port, or credible jailbreak promise has surfaced for either device. Every active guide explicitly excludes them: the most current community reflash guide (August 2026) warns that it covers only the 2019 first gen and that "everything Amazon sells new today is different hardware — both the 2023 3rd generation and the 2025 4th generation — and none of this works on either" ([localsmarthomeguide](https://localsmarthomeguide.com/articles/echo-show-8-to-home-assistant-smart-display/)); Derek Seaman's continuously updated jailbreak guide states flatly that "NO OTHER Echo Show models OR generations are supported" beyond the Show 5 Gen 1/2 and Show 8 Gen 1 ([Derek Seaman](https://www.derekseaman.com/2025/11/home-assistant-hacking-your-echo-show-5-and-8.html)); and when a Show 11 owner asked about sideloading in 2026, the community answer was that only the 2019–2021 devices qualify ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sds6mo/snapshots/2026-04-07T00%3A00%3A00.5316Z)). The researchers with demonstrated skills on Echo hardware — the amonet lineage (R0rt1z2), the Gen 3 hardware rooter (XDA user "Vowed"), and the Show 15 forensic pair (Crasselt & Pugliese) — have published nothing on AZ3 or Vega hardware, and the DFRWS 2024 paper's single forward-looking remark on Vega remains an open question rather than a result ([arXiv:2408.15768](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15768)). Demand exists (the sideload questions keep coming), so the silence reflects genuine difficulty plus platform newness, not disinterest.

The OS itself is equally untouched. **No CVE, disclosure, ZDI advisory, conference talk, or academic paper targets Vega OS** despite it shipping to consumers since May 2023 on the Echo Show 5 3rd gen ([AFTVnews](https://www.aftvnews.com/amazons-in-house-vega-os-is-already-on-the-new-echo-show-5/)) and powering Amazon's flagship devices since late 2025; CVE-database sweeps for "Vega OS", "AZ2", "AZ3", and "Echo Show" surface only legacy items (CVE-2018-11567, CVE-2022-25809, ZDI-20-537) predating the platform ([NVD CVE-2022-25809](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2022-25809), [ZDI-20-537](https://www.zerodayinitiative.com/advisories/ZDI-20-537/)). One structural reason deserves emphasis: **Amazon's Vulnerability Research Program pays up to $30,000 for Echo-device findings** (scope explicitly covers all Echo hardware "running the latest available software"; ~$800K paid, 310 reports in the last 90 days as of this research), so a professional who finds a Vega flaw earns more by disclosing privately than by releasing a free jailbreak — the exact inverse of the boot-ROM-on-end-of-life-hardware economics that produced the Gen 1 unlock wave ([HackerOne: amazonvrp-devices](https://hackerone.com/amazonvrp-devices)). "No bug bounty results" is therefore only provable as "no *published* results"; private VRP activity on Vega is likely and invisible. On the hardware side the void is just as total: **no teardown of MD6NE4 exists anywhere** — iFixit and TechInsights have not touched it, no FCC internal-photo set has been extracted, and no board photo, UART pinout, or eMMC test-point map has been published ([TechInsights Gen 3 teardown, for contrast](https://www.techinsights.com/blog/deep-dive-teardown-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-r855d6-smart-display)).

## Vega OS deleted the attack surface every Echo exploit used

Every hack in the Echo Show's history exploited Android. Gen 1's amonet chain rode MediaTek's boot ROM into fastboot and TWRP; Gen 3's hardware root used its eMMC write to enable ADB over serial, inject ADB keys into `/data/misc/adb`, and `pivot_root` into Debian — all Android/Fire OS machinery ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346), [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/07/08/hacking-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-via-uart-and-emmc/)). The 2025 model removes that machinery outright: it runs **Vega OS, Amazon's in-house Linux-based, non-Android operating system** — confirmed by converging evidence including TechRadar's review ("Alexa and VegaOS generally run well"), an Amazon-staffed forum thread referencing "the Show 8 (2025) with Amazon's Vega OS", Amazon's developer device tables omitting it from every Fire OS/Android spec list, and a differently-named GPL source drop matching the Vega release train ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/home/smart-home-hubs/echo-show-8-4th-gen-2025-review), [Amazon Forum](https://www.amazonforum.com/s/question/0D5at00000hAmCmCAK/help-echo-show-8-silk-browser), [Amazon developer docs](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/device-specs/device-specifications-echo-show.html)). Vega's public internals describe a Linux userland with glibc 2.35, per-app linker-namespace isolation, VPKG packages (not APKs), and a React Native–based "KeplerScript" app runtime ([Amazon Vega docs](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/vega/0.23/linker-namespacing.html)) — a small, purpose-built OS with **no Android runtime, no consumer sideloading, and no ADB**. On the Fire TV line, where AFTVnews has tracked it closely, Vega killed sideloading, the Downloader app, custom launchers, and the "unknown sources" toggle by design ([AFTVnews](https://www.aftvnews.com/these-are-the-fire-tvs-that-dont-support-sideloading-or-downloader-due-to-vega-os-replacing-fire-os/)).

Two nuances keep this from being a perfectly blank wall. First, an August 2026 test-lab analysis found that Vega ships **`vda`, a renamed but protocol-identical `adb`** — same wire protocol, same `~/.android/adbkey` RSA keys, drivable by stock Google platform-tools — meaning the entire mature adb attack surface conceptually applies to Vega devices ([RobusTest](https://robustest.com/blog/the-one-line-fork)). But every documented gate around it (developer account, on-device auth code, USB) is **Fire TV–only**: Amazon's "Enable Developer Mode" documentation (v0.24, August 2026) is written entirely for Fire TV, and **no Echo Show model appears anywhere in the Vega developer documentation** — no dev mode, no VDA, no sideload path is documented to exist on any Vega Echo Show ([Vega docs: developer mode](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/vega/0.24/developer-mode.html), [AFTVnews](https://www.aftvnews.com/sideloading-is-truly-dead-on-the-fire-tv-stick-4k-select-with-vega-os/)). Whether the Show 8's build even contains the `vda` listener is unpublished. Second, Amazon never retrofits OS families — existing Fire OS devices keep Fire OS, so the lockdown is a property of new hardware only, and there is no sign Amazon plans to open an Echo dev program on Vega ([AFTVnews](https://www.aftvnews.com/these-are-the-fire-tvs-that-dont-support-sideloading-or-downloader-due-to-vega-os-replacing-fire-os/)). The net effect for hackability: the soft targets that enabled every previous Echo hack — APK installation, ADB daemons, launcher replacement, fastboot — are architecturally absent, not merely disabled.

## The microphone and camera answer to Alexa alone

On stock firmware the access question has a hard, simple answer: **no third-party surface reaches the hardware**. The 2025 Show 8's 13 MP auto-framing camera and five-microphone far-field array (up from four on Gen 3) feed the AZ3 Pro's on-device AI stack — Amazon's Omnisense sensor fusion running wake-word detection, Visual ID, and presence — and Amazon exposes none of it to developers ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/amazon-new-echo-devices-alexa-plus), [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/best-amazon-echo-show)). The only programmatic sensor API in the entire Alexa toolkit, the Web API for Games' Entity-Sensing and Smart-Motion extensions, delivers derived motion data, is restricted to "motion-capable" devices (Show 10 and Astro), and is session-bound — the Show 8 is not motion-capable, and the skill session "remains open until the user ends the game" with no autostart, background execution, or persistence ([Amazon developer docs](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/docs/alexa/web-api-for-games/alexa-games-about.html)). Alexa+ add-ons, the newest third-party surface, are cloud-hosted conversational integrations limited to select partners — they run nothing on the device at all ([Alexa+ developer docs](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/alexaplus/add-ons/home.html)). Even the privacy hardware weakened: Gen 3's mechanical camera shutter is gone on the 2025 model, replaced by a single electronic "Microphones & Camera Enable/Disable" button, with the camera enabled by default — a software-interpreted path, not a documented hard disconnect ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Echo_Show), [Amazon help](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=T57xOIjRG5FLoczPDn)).

The only remote camera surfaces are Amazon's own, and both are first-party and cloud-routed: **Drop In** (two-way audio/video that connects automatically with a green indicator, no documented session cap) and **Home Monitoring / Live View** (one-way feed viewed from the Alexa app), both confirmed supported on the 2025 Show 8 and Show 11 by dedicated Amazon help pages ([Amazon help: Drop In](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GS3WRTSRKD2U6MCK), [Amazon help: Home Monitoring](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=G3ZTXBS783ML96QB), [Amazon help: live feed on Show 8/11 2025](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=TmLfDvoCD4PHdW8pcb)). Neither is scriptable by third parties, offers no RTSP/ONVIF/local stream for NVR software, and avoids the cloud only never. The closest stock approximation of a 24/7 custom app ranks, in descending order of persistence: the Amazon Photos photo frame (truly indefinite), the Alexa+ adaptive ambient home screen and first-party widgets (indefinite but Amazon-controlled content), the voice-openable full-screen smart-home dashboard (stays up while open; third-party camera feeds shown on it auto-stop at ~5–10 minutes per vendor caps — Blink 5, Wyze and Ring ~10), and a dashboard loaded in the frozen Vega build of the Silk browser, which inherits the same auto-close/no-autostart problems documented on Fire OS Shows with no sideload fallback to fix them ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/getting-started-echo-show-8-11-alexa-plus-features), [Wyze forum](https://forums.wyze.com/t/extend-duration-of-livestream-via-alexa-to-more-than-10-minutes/5683), [Amazon Forum (Blink)](https://www.amazonforum.com/s/question/0D56Q0000A7PiMsSQK/alexa-show-driveway-gives-me-6-minutes-of-the-blink-driveway-camera-view), [Amazon Forum: Silk](https://www.amazonforum.com/s/question/0D5at00000hAmCmCAK/help-echo-show-8-silk-browser)). Those vendor caps are themselves a bad omen: Amazon demonstrably will not allow indefinite continuous streams on Echo screens from non-Amazon sources.

## The one open door is a soldering iron, and nobody has walked through it

The silicon underneath is a question mark that matters. Amazon markets AZ3 Pro as its own "custom-designed" full SoC with an integrated AI accelerator — a step up from Gen 3, where the AZ2 was merely a neural engine beside an Amlogic application processor ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/amazon-new-echo-devices-alexa-plus), [PCMag](https://me.pcmag.com/en/smart-displays/33403/amazon-echo-show-8-4th-gen-2025)) — but **no die-level or even board-level teardown exists**, and every "custom silicon" claim in circulation traces back to Amazon's own launch materials ([security notes](../research_notes/Echo%20Show%208%202025%20hackability/security_research.md)). The one documented fact about lineage points away from Gen 3's platform: the AZ-series descends from the **Amazon–MediaTek AZ1 collaboration built around the MT8512**, not from Amlogic, so the Amlogic conventions the Gen 3 exploit leaned on (U-Boot serial autoboot, Amlogic boot-ROM behavior, Android fastboot/recovery partitions) have no documented equivalent on AZ3 Pro ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2020/9/24/21453205/amazon-az1-neural-edge-processor-echo-show-10-alexa-voice-on-device)). A community device database lists the 2025 Show 8's core chip as "Amazon Azma15? (AZ3 Pro NPU)" — with its own uncertainty marker — and its codename as "madeleine" ([connect-the-dots wiki](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/gilderchuck/connect-the-dots/wiki)); whether "Azma15" is a derived MediaTek-family part like AZ1 was is unconfirmed.

The physical door that remains is eMMC. The 2025 Show 8 still specs **8 GB of eMMC storage** — no documented move to encrypted eUFS or in-package storage — so a chip-off dump/write of the kind that cracked Gen 3 is physically plausible in principle ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/smart-home/echo-show-8-4th-gen-review)). But the honest classification is **theoretical transferability only**: nobody has published even step one (locating the pads on the new mainboard), and the parts of the Gen 3 technique that made it *useful* are Android artifacts that do not exist on Vega — there is no ADB to enable over serial, no `/data/misc/adb` key file to inject, no unverified `/odm` partition to hide a setuid binary in, and it is unknown whether Vega encrypts its filesystem at rest or whether the AZ3 Pro boot chain honors offline eMMC modifications at all ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/07/08/hacking-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-via-uart-and-emmc/), [arXiv:2408.15768](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15768)). Even the Show 15 forensic researchers' hopeful scenario — their eMMC pinout "could potentially become handy if Vega is rolled out on older devices" — inverted in practice: Vega ships only on new silicon, so their hardware work would have to be redone from scratch on a mainboard nobody has photographed ([arXiv:2408.15768](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15768)).

The most consequential open door costs nothing to open: **Amazon publishes a GPL source tarball for the 2025 Show 8 — `Echo_Show_8_Source_Release_1571.tar.gz` — that no one has publicly inspected** ([Amazon Source Code Notice](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=201626480)). That archive almost certainly contains the AZ3 Pro kernel source, device tree, and configuration: it would reveal the kernel version, dm-verity/verified-boot flags, and whether the mic array and camera are exposed as standard ALSA/V4L2 nodes — the difference between a future custom-Linux port being "hard" and "blind." It is the single cheapest, highest-value next step in the entire assessment, ahead of pulling the FCC internal photos for MD6NE4 and ahead of a board-level teardown for UART/eMMC pads. If a first breakthrough ever comes, the probable sequence is: teardown → forensic eMMC dump (which would itself constitute the first public Vega OS security research) → write-path and persistence questions — and the one-sided patch dynamics that governed prior platforms apply here too: any software-tier Vega flaw is OTA-patchable within weeks (Amazon patched the Pro-me3us Fire TV exploit in about a month), while hardware eMMC access is forever ([Liliputing](https://liliputing.com/amazon-patches-the-exploit-that-supercharged-fire-tv-and-fire-tablet-hacking)). Realistic prospects: **moderate for hardware forensics within 1–3 years, low for a durable software jailbreak** — the first "root" of a 2025 Show, if it comes, will look like soldering, not an exploit zip.

## Four generations, one natural experiment in platform rotation

Set against its three ancestors, the 2025 model completes a clean inversion: each generation is less hackable than the one before it, and for the first time the cause is deliberate.

| | Gen 1 (2019, "crown") | Gen 2 (2021, "athena") | Gen 3 (2023, "rhodes") | Gen 4 (2025, "madeleine") |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Silicon | MediaTek MT8163 | MediaTek MT8183 | Amlogic Pop1 + AZ2 | AZ3 Pro (unpublished; MediaTek-lineage plausible) |
| OS | Fire OS 6 (Android) | Fire OS 7 (Android) | Fire OS 7 (Android) | Vega OS (Linux, non-Android) |
| Unlock | **amonet boot-ROM exploit, permanent** | None, ever | Hardware-only: UART + eMMC write to unverified /odm | **Nothing, at any tier** |
| Best achievable | LineageOS 18.1, TWRP, /e/OS | Silk browser workarounds | Root + Debian via pivot_root (Amazon kernel) | Stock only; no third-party surface persists |
| Why | Inherited Fire-tablet boot-ROM flaw | Accidental SoC escape | Fuse-locked boot, off-chip eMMC gap | Platform rotation: new silicon + new OS + no research |

Gen 1 remains the anomaly that proves the rule: its MT8163 boot-ROM flaw lives in mask ROM, no OTA can revoke it, and LineageOS 18.1 builds were still maintained into late 2026 ([R0rt1z2/amonet](https://github.com/R0rt1z2/amonet), [amazon-oss/releases](https://github.com/amazon-oss/releases/releases)). Gen 2 was stranded by accident — "a lost cause, at least for now, since it uses a different processor… and Amazon also used different signing keys" ([R0rt1z2 on Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/01/02/jailbreaking-the-amazon-echo-show/#comment-8233198)). Gen 3 was locked at the fuse level and yielded only to a soldering iron ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346)). The 2025 model closes all three openings at once: no shared boot-ROM lineage to inherit an exploit from, no Android userland to hijack even with physical access, and a security-research vacuum with paid incentives (the VRP) pulling any discoveries into private disclosure. Notably, the amonet exploit chain itself could never have transferred anyway — it targets MT8163-class boot ROMs, and the vulnerability was patched in production as far back as March 2019 ([XDA cronos FAQ](https://xdaforums.com/t/unlock-root-twrp-unbrick-amazon-echo-show-5-2nd-gen-2021-cronos.4772596/)); Amazon also demonstrably disabled the BROM USBDL path in later hardware revisions of the 2019 line, a documented precedent for hardening boot-ROM access when exploits surface ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Echo_Show)).

## Conclusion

For the question "can it *ever* run a 24/7 custom app with full mic and camera access" the honest answer splits by tier. On stock firmware: **never on the current architecture** — not because a feature is missing but because the app model has no concept of third-party hardware access or persistence, and Amazon's trajectory (killing sideloading, freezing Silk, gating every Vega dev surface behind Fire TV) points the other way. With a future hardware break: **theoretically yes, but nothing enabling it exists today** — even a successful eMMC-level takeover would still face a presumably signed boot chain, unknown filesystem encryption, a proprietary Omnisense audio/vision stack on an undocumented NPU, and vendor kernel drivers that no one has yet laid eyes on inside the unexamined 1571 source tarball. The mic and camera question specifically turns on whether that tarball exposes standard ALSA/V4L2 interfaces; until someone looks, the honest status is unknowable, and no one has looked.

The change from the last report is categorical: the Echo Show 8's hackability was previously an accident of procurement, varying wildly by generation; with the 2025 model it became policy. The practical reading for anyone choosing hardware — an always-on recorder, a Home Assistant dashboard, anything needing raw sensors — is unchanged from the earlier conclusion and now firmer: the 2019 Gen 1 is the permanent open device, the 2023 Gen 3 is the soldering-iron Linux option, and the 2025 model should be treated as a closed appliance for at least the next one to two years, with three cheap probes — the 1571 tarball, the FCC internal photos, and a mainboard teardown — capable of moving that estimate the moment anyone performs them.
