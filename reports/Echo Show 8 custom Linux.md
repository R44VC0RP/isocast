# Jailbreaking three generations of Echo Show 8

Yes — the Echo Show 8 has been reverse-engineered and runs custom software today, but the answer splits cleanly along generational lines. The **1st Gen (2019, codename "crown", MediaTek MT8163)** is fully unlocked: XDA developer R0rt1z2 ported the amonet boot-ROM exploit chain to it in **November 2025**, yielding a permanent bootloader unlock, TWRP recovery, and shipping unofficial **LineageOS 18.1** and **/e/OS** builds that permanently wipe Fire OS ([R0rt1z2/amonet](https://github.com/R0rt1z2/amonet), [XDA crown thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/unlock-root-twrp-unbrick-amazon-echo-show-8-1st-gen-2019-crown.4766687/)). The **3rd Gen (2023, codename "rhodes", Amlogic "Pop1")** was rooted in 2026 by a **hardware-only route** — soldering to UART and eMMC pads to write a setuid-root image to the unverified /odm partition, then `pivot_root` into a full Debian or Alpine userspace — but its fuse-burned secure boot means a custom kernel is impossible ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346), [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/07/08/hacking-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-via-uart-and-emmc/)). The **2nd Gen (2021, codename "athena", MediaTek MT8183)** is the orphan: its SoC is not vulnerable to amonet, no exploit has ever been published for it, and it remains locked with no realistic path ([R0rt1z2 on Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/01/02/jailbreaking-the-amazon-echo-show/#comment-8233198)). For an owner today, the practical takeaway is simple: a used Gen 1 (~$75) is one of the cheapest hackable Android smart displays available; a Gen 3 can run real Linux but only with board-level soldering; a Gen 2 should be treated as a closed appliance ([Derek Seaman guide](https://www.derekseaman.com/2025/11/home-assistant-hacking-your-echo-show-5-and-8.html)).

## Gen 1 (2019): a boot-ROM exploit made it a fully unlockable Android tablet

The Echo Show 8 Gen 1 is, structurally, a Fire tablet with a screen-first launcher: it runs **Fire OS 6 on an Android 7 base** (firmware package `com.amazon.crown.android.os`), on a **MediaTek MT8163** quad-core Cortex-A53 with Mali-450 graphics, **2 GB RAM and 8 GB eMMC** ([FTVDB](https://ftvdb.com/echo/firmware/com.amazon.crown.android.os), [community install guide](https://github.com/winmutt/self-2026/blob/master/LINEAGEOS_INSTALLATION_GUIDE.md)). That silicon choice turned out to be decisive. The MT8163 is the same platform as Amazon's 2018 Fire HD 8/10 tablets, for which xyzz's **amonet** exploit chain — a mask-ROM (BROM) bug plus a preloader exploit plus a fastboot buffer overflow — had existed since early 2019 ([xyzz/amonet](https://github.com/xyzz/amonet), [Firmware Security](https://firmwaresecurity.com/2019/01/31/amonet-a-bootrom-exploit-for-mediatek-devices)). For six years nobody ported it to the Echo Show: the devices differ in preloader builds and partition layout, and before November 2025 **no public root existed for any Echo Show** — the strongest documented results were a patched userland Chromium exploit on the Echo Show 5 at Pwn2Own Tokyo 2019 (ZDI-20-537, $60,000 prize, fixed by April 2020) and patched cloud-level Alexa web bugs from Check Point in 2020 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/11/09/browser-bug-amazon-echo-hack/), [ZDI-20-537](https://www.zerodayinitiative.com/advisories/ZDI-20-537/), [Check Point](https://research.checkpoint.com/2020/amazons-alexa-hacked/)).

R0rt1z2's port changed that. Released **November 2025** on the `mt8163-echo-show` branch of his amonet fork, the chain unlocks the bootloader of the Echo Show 8 Gen 1 ("crown") alongside the Echo Show 5 Gen 1 ("checkers") and, since January 2026, the Echo Show 5 Gen 2 ("cronos") ([R0rt1z2/amonet](https://github.com/R0rt1z2/amonet), [AFTVnews](https://www.aftvnews.com/amazons-older-echo-show-5-echo-spot-and-echo-dot-have-been-rooted-with-unlocked-bootloaders-and-custom-lineageos-roms/)). Because Amazon uses per-device signing keys, the unlock works by cross-flashing a **vulnerable Echo Show 5 demo-unit bootloader** and chainloading Show 5 firmware components onto the Show 8 — a quirk that initially left only 1 GB of the physical 2 GB RAM visible, until amonet v2.0.0 "unlocked the extra 1 GB of RAM, giving us a total of 2 GB" ([XDA crown thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/unlock-root-twrp-unbrick-amazon-echo-show-8-1st-gen-2019-crown.4766687/)). The result is a manufactured "hacked fastboot," **TWRP 3.7.0**, button-remapped boot modes (Mute = hacked fastboot, Vol-Up = recovery), and an intentionally insecure preloader USBDL port that makes most bricks recoverable with MTKClient ([XDA crown thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/unlock-root-twrp-unbrick-amazon-echo-show-8-1st-gen-2019-crown.4766687/)).

On top of that unlock, bengris32 ships **unofficial LineageOS 18.1 (Android 11)** for crown, built on kernel **4.9.337** repurposed from Amazon's own Fire OS 7 MT8163 tablet source drop, with device trees and builds distributed through the `amazon-oss` GitHub org ([XDA LineageOS thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/rom-unofficial-11-crown-lineageos-18-1-for-the-amazon-echo-show-8-2019.4766709/), [amazon-oss/releases](https://github.com/amazon-oss/releases/releases)). An unofficial **/e/OS R build** and **full camera enablement patches** followed in early 2026 ([/e/OS community](https://community.e.foundation/t/unofficial-build-amazon-echo-show-8-2019-crown-for-e-os-r-2-3/77270), [jxlarrea/lineageos-echo-show-camera](https://github.com/jxlarrea/lineageos-echo-show-camera)). Real-world adoption is documented across multiple independent sources: Derek Seaman runs four Lineage Echo Shows (two of them Show 8 Gen 1) as Home Assistant dashboards, and the HA community reports Lineage on Show 8 Gen 1 "works great with HA, spotify, bubblepnp" ([Derek Seaman](https://www.derekseaman.com/2025/11/home-assistant-hacking-your-echo-show-5-and-8.html), [HA community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/echo-show-5-1st-gen-6-5-7-0-6-5-7-1-root/947524)).

The durability of this path is its most remarkable property. The entry-point flaw lives in **mask ROM, which no OTA update can rewrite**; Amazon's only patchable surfaces are the later preloader/LK stages. Consistent with that, users unlocked Show 8 Gen 1 units on the **latest Fire OS** in November 2025, Fire OS 6.5.7.3 was still exploitable in July 2026, and Derek Seaman's guide remained current with updated supported-firmware versions (6.5.7.1–6.5.7.4) as of **October 4, 2026** — a year after release, with no Amazon patch landing ([XDA ROM thread p.3](https://xdaforums.com/t/rom-unofficial-11-crown-lineageos-18-1-for-the-amazon-echo-show-8-2019.4766709/page-3), [Derek Seaman](https://www.derekseaman.com/2025/11/home-assistant-hacking-your-echo-show-5-and-8.html)). This contrasts sharply with Amazon's handling of *software*-tier exploits like mtk-su (CVE-2020-0069, patched March 2020) and the Pro-me3us system-user exploit (patched within a month in Fire OS 8.1.5.3, October 2025) — patchable bugs get patched fast; boot-ROM footholds do not ([CVE-2020-0069](https://notcve.org/cve/CVE-2020-0069), [Liliputing](https://liliputing.com/amazon-patches-the-exploit-that-supercharged-fire-tv-and-fire-tablet-hacking)).

## Gen 2 (2021): the locked middle child nobody ever cracked

The 2nd Gen (model A8H3N2, codename "athena") sits in the worst position of the three. It moved to a **MediaTek MT8183 octa-core** with 2 GB DDR3 and 8 GB storage, running Fire OS 7 on Android 9 ([B&H spec sheet](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/1642766-REG/amazon_b084dcjksl_echo_show_8_2g.html), [FTVDB athena](https://ftvdb.com/echo/firmware/com.amazon.athena.android.os)). The SoC change was accidental good security: the amonet chain exploits a bug specific to MT8163-class boot ROMs, and R0rt1z2 stated flatly that "the **Echo Show 8 2nd Generation is not supported, as it uses a different SoC that is not vulnerable to this exploit**" ([R0rt1z2 on Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/01/02/jailbreaking-the-amazon-echo-show/#comment-8233198)). An XDA FAQ confirms the boundary: "the exploit only affects devices with the MediaTek MT8163 SoC" ([XDA](https://xdaforums.com/t/echo-show-2nd-generation-possible-to-unlock-similar-to-echo-show-8-1st-gen.4771627/)).

Nothing has replaced amonet for athena. There is **no public unlock, no root, no published hardware-debug documentation** — no UART pinout, no eMMC points, no `fastboot getvar` dump, because nobody has ever unlocked one to dump it ([hardware research survey](https://xdaforums.com/t/amazon-echo-show-8-2nd.4777173)). The device's own bootloader ships locked with no official unlock mechanism; Amazon has never offered `fastboot oem unlock` on any Echo Show, and the community tracker lists no path for it ([bootloader-unlock-wall-of-shame](https://github.com/zenfyrdev/bootloader-unlock-wall-of-shame/blob/main/brands/amazon/README.md)). A theoretical door exists — MT8183 appears in third-party analyses of MediaTek preloader CVEs (CVE-2023-20694/20695/20725 list it among affected SoCs) — but no exploit has been demonstrated, and the 10th-generation-plus Fire tablets on the same generation of silicon have likewise never been rooted ([mouseos CVE analysis](https://github.com/mouseos/mediatek-mt8365-preloader-exploit/blob/main/docs/CVE-2023-20694/exploit_analysis.md), [blackhathq](https://www.blackhathq.com/post/how-to-jailbreak-amazon-fire-tablet)). An owner's realistic options on Gen 2 today are the no-root workarounds: dashboards in the Silk browser, custom Alexa skills that open URLs, and voice-triggered re-launch automations — all of which fight the same enemy, Silk's **10–15 minute auto-close and lack of any kiosk mode** ([gethomepage discussion](https://github.com/gethomepage/homepage/discussions/2853), [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/how-i-turned-my-echo-show-into-a-home-assistant-control-panel/)).

## Gen 3 (2023): root demands a soldering iron, and the kernel stays Amazon's

The 3rd Gen (model R85SD6, codename "rhodes") is the most interesting case: it is the only Echo Show 8 running genuine full GNU/Linux userspace — and the only one where getting there requires opening the case. Amazon switched to a custom-branded **Amlogic "P0P1-R" / PopcornA "Pop1"** octa-core part with the AZ2 neural engine, 2 GB LPDDR4, and 8 GB NAND ([TechInsights teardown](https://www.techinsights.com/blog/deep-dive-teardown-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-r855d6-smart-display), [Amazon dev docs](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/device-specs/device-specifications-echo-show.html)). Its boot chain is locked at the deepest level available to Amazon: **secure boot burned into the chip via eFuses**, an X.509 certificate check on boot images, dm-verity on /system and /vendor, fastboot and recovery blocked by policy, and **kexec compiled out of the kernel** — so custom kernels are impossible without an Amlogic boot-ROM-level exploit that nobody has found ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346)). With the eMMC removed, the board traps at EL3 failing secure boot — the lock is in silicon, not software ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346)).

The 2026 break, by XDA user Vowed, goes around the chip instead of through it. The method: disassemble the device (a job Hackaday calls daunting even by iFixit's guide), cut open the eMMC shield, and solder or pogo-pin to the **8 GB BGA-153 eMMC pads** — the only way to write partitions, since "fastboot is fully blocked on this device" ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346), [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/07/08/hacking-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-via-uart-and-emmc/), [iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Amazon+Echo+Show+8+3rd+Gen+Display+Assembly+Replacement/179345)). The off-chip write targets the one weak partition: **/odm (8 MiB, ext4) has no dm-verity and no `nosuid` mount flag**. Writing an image there containing a setuid-root binary, a permissive SELinux CIL policy, and a tee-supplicant library hook yields `adb shell /odm/bin/su` → uid=0, surviving reboots ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346)). From root, the researcher demonstrated **`pivot_root` into Debian or Alpine** ("Debian-Debilexa"), including a ported Wayland DRM driver running **GNOME** on the stock locked kernel ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346), [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/07/08/hacking-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-via-uart-and-emmc/)).

The limits are real, though. This is a **hardware exploit with no software shortcut**: it needs an eMMC reader/programmer, fine soldering or a pogo rig (wires under ~5 cm), and the whole procedure is brick-prone; an OTA update that reimages /odm or tightens SELinux policy would require re-soldering to restore root ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346)). JTAG pins physically exist and the firmware's JTAG_ON SMC is honored, but debug authentication is locked by the bootloader, so JTAG "does not work" ([XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346)). On the plus side, the technique is transferable: the same Pop1 processor powers the Echo Show 15/21 line, a forensic repo documents the identical UART/eMMC procedure for the Show 15, and the XDA author notes "the same can be applied to a newer Echo 15 with the same processor" ([jcrasselt/amazon-echo-show-15](https://github.com/jcrasselt/amazon-echo-show-15), [XDA rhodes thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/root-system-hardware-amazon-echo-show-8-3rd-gen-rhodes-pop1-via-uart-and-emmc.4793346)).

## What an owner should actually do today

The practical decision matrix, per generation:

| | Gen 1 (2019, crown) | Gen 2 (2021, athena) | Gen 3 (2023, rhodes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unlock | **amonet software unlock** (USB, no soldering) | None, ever | None possible in software |
| Best achievable | LineageOS 18.1 / /e/OS, TWRP, Magisk root | Silk browser + skills workarounds | Root + Debian/Alpine via pivot_root on stock kernel |
| Effort | Windows PC + micro-USB, ~an afternoon | Zero-hack workarounds only | Open case, cut eMMC shield, solder/pogo eMMC pads |
| End state | Not an Amazon device anymore | Still fully an Amazon device | GNU/Linux userspace, Amazon kernel, Alexa gone |
| Main risks | Brick if TEE/LK/preloader flashed wrong; firmware window 6.5.7.1–6.5.7.4 | OTA removes nothing to lose — nothing was gained | Brick risk high; OTA may require re-soldering |

For a Gen 1 owner, the path is documented end-to-end in Derek Seaman's continuously updated guide (Nov 2025 – Oct 2026): verify the device is on **Fire OS 6.5.7.1–6.5.7.4**, keep Wi-Fi disconnected during the procedure so no OTA lands mid-process, boot to stock fastboot (all three buttons), run `fastbrick.bat` over micro-USB, then optionally flash LineageOS 18.1 permanently — or stop at the jailbreak and merely sideload apps and a custom launcher over ADB while keeping Alexa ([Derek Seaman](https://www.derekseaman.com/2025/11/home-assistant-hacking-your-echo-show-5-and-8.html), [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/how-i-turned-my-echo-show-into-a-home-assistant-control-panel/)). The middle path is worth considering: jailbroken-but-not-wiped Fire OS keeps voice features while allowing Fully Kiosk Browser or the Home Assistant app; flashing LineageOS eliminates the OTA risk entirely at the cost of Alexa ([Derek Seaman](https://www.derekseaman.com/2025/11/home-assistant-hacking-your-echo-show-5-and-8.html)). Realistic expectations for the end state: an **8 GB storage** ceiling, a kernel-4.9 Android that "lacks a lot of features required to run modern Android," deep sleep disabled, and occasional camera and audio-HAL bugs on current builds ([XDA LineageOS thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/rom-unofficial-11-crown-lineageos-18-1-for-the-amazon-echo-show-8-2019.4766709/)). Android Authority's hands-on on the sibling Show 5 found a fresh Lineage install "surprisingly lean… faster zipping around here than it ever did on Amazon's own Fire OS," with the camera non-functional on that build ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/echo-show-5-jailbreak-lineageos-3636721/)).

For anyone buying hardware for this purpose, the Gen 1 is the clear target: used units ran ~$75 or less on eBay in late 2025, the community scene is active (dedicated XDA threads, maintained builds through September 2026, an /e/OS port, multiple independent long-form guides), and the boot-ROM foothold cannot be revoked by any update Amazon ships for the remaining life of the hardware ([Derek Seaman](https://www.derekseaman.com/2025/11/home-assistant-hacking-your-echo-show-5-and-8.html), [FTVDB crown](https://ftvdb.com/echo/firmware/com.amazon.crown.android.os)). The motivation is often less tinkerlust than annoyance: Echo Show devices began showing **non-disableable advertisements**, which is repeatedly cited as the top reason owners wipe Fire OS ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/01/02/jailbreaking-the-amazon-echo-show/), [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/echo-show-5-jailbreak-lineageos-3636721/)).

## The ceiling: Android ROMs yes, mainline Linux not yet

It is worth being precise about what "custom Linux" means here, because none of the three generations runs a truly custom kernel. Gen 1's demonstrated ceiling is **Android 11 on a 4.9.337 kernel** repurposed from Amazon's own GPL source drop; a literal mainline-Linux Echo Show 8 remains research-grade — the mt8163-mainline project on the sibling Fire-tablet silicon has serial console, simplefb, and early Panfrost GPU support working, but no complete port, and postmarketOS on the comparable Fire 7 tablet is only partial ([XDA LineageOS thread](https://xdaforums.com/t/rom-unofficial-11-crown-lineageos-18-1-for-the-amazon-echo-show-8-2019.4766709/), [mt8163-mainline/linux](https://github.com/mt8163-mainline/linux), [postmarketOS wiki](https://wiki.postmarketos.org/wiki/Amazon_Fire_7_2019_%28amazon-mustang%29)). Gen 3's Debian/GNOME demonstration runs a **full GNU userspace under Amazon's locked kernel** — arguably closer to "running Linux" than Gen 1's Android, but with the kernel permanently out of the owner's control. Gen 2 has neither. The one precedent for true kernel-level custom Linux on a consumer smart display is the Google Nest Hub 2nd Gen, where a U-Boot USB flaw allowed booting Ubuntu 22.04 in 2022 — and Google OTA-patched the window shut within weeks, illustrating that bootloader-stage flaws are closable while mask-ROM flaws are not ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2022/06/20/breaking-google-nest-hubs-secure-boot/), [Liliputing](https://liliputing.com/hacker-installs-ubuntu-on-a-google-nest-hub-2nd-gen-smart-display)).

## Conclusion

The Echo Show 8's story is really a natural experiment in how one product line's security fate is decided by silicon procurement rather than engineering intent. Gen 1 inherited the Fire tablets' exploitable MT8163 boot ROM and, with it, a permanent unlock that Amazon cannot revoke six years after the chip shipped; Gen 2's move to MT8183 accidentally stranded it; Gen 3's Amlogic platform was deliberately locked at the fuse level, forcing researchers below the OS entirely. The result is an inversion of what a manufacturer would want: the oldest, cheapest, weakest device is the most open, and the newest is open only to people with hot-air rework stations.

Two forward-looking implications stand out. First, the window for the Gen 1 path is bounded not by patches but by hardware attrition — the BROM flaw dies with the last working 2019 board, so the community's "buy and jailbreak ASAP" advice is about firmware windows and supply, not a closing exploit. Second, Amazon's migration of newer Echo hardware to its non-Android **Vega OS** (already shipping on the 2025 Echo Show 5) ends the LineageOS-style pathway on future devices entirely — the Fire OS era that made Gen 1 hackable is ending, which makes the 2019 unit a finite and arguably final artifact of the hackable Echo era ([AFTVnews](https://www.aftvnews.com/amazons-in-house-vega-os-is-already-on-the-new-echo-show-5), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Echo_Show)).
