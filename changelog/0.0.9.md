## Major
### Export a take for your editor
The in-app editor, timelines and recording targets are removed; existing `.isoprj` files stay untouched but no longer open in Isocast. `isocast project export-nle` writes a take as a Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or OpenTimelineIO project, with every ISO source on its own track, pointing at the original files. Nothing is re-encoded or copied.
Icon: icons/take-export.svg
Links: [CLI reference](../docs/cli.md#isocast-project-export-nle)
### Control Isocast on another Mac (early)
Settings › Remote pairs this Mac with another one on the local network, and the other Mac's engine opens in a window of its own. Its program plays as live video, with sound once you turn on the program monitor's speaker.
Icon: icons/remote-control.svg
Links: [Remote engines](../docs/remote-engines.md)
### Screen colour depth
Settings › Recording › ISO Recording chooses 8-bit (the default, smaller files) or 10-bit HEVC (smoother gradients). It applies to all ISO files; a source's Properties can override it.
Icon: icons/colour-depth.svg
## Improvements
- Remote viewing does less CPU work: resizing reuses its GPU setup between pictures and decoded video stays on the Mac's GPU. Remote and local pictures use the same colour handling.
- Remote video supports clients that need H.264 Constrained Baseline, without changing recording profiles.
- On a busy Mac with a 30 fps canvas, previews step down to 15 fps sooner. Engines using software graphics pause previews during recording or streaming.
- `isocast api spec --json` describes every engine method as an OpenRPC document.
- A like-for-like comparison with OBS Studio 32.2.2 (1080p60 program plus one ISO file, M4 Max, development build): 43.8% of one core and 208 MiB against OBS's 97.2% and 1,192 MiB.
## Fixes
- Remote sound recovers from lost network packets without waiting through a long gap.
- 10-bit HEVC screen files keep the screen's colours instead of shifting in tone.
